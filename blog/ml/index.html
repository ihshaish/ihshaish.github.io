Machine Learning

Essays on how learning systems work and how they fail. Each piece is grounded in the established literature, references the papers it leans on, and comes with a notebook you can run yourself whenever a claim can be shown rather than asserted.

The paper rulebook: writing and review rules that earned their place

4 September 2026 · 7 min read · includes two animated examples

Seven review rounds of one manuscript left us with a ruleset: which words we banned and why, the three-beat paragraph that replaced the sentence doing six jobs, how claims get scoped to exactly what their tables show, the figure grammar, and the review process that catches what we miss. Also an argument that a ruleset maintained this way is itself a continual learning system, replay buffer included.

Continual learning: what it is, and what I've learned working with it

28 July 2026 · 19 min read · includes a notebook

This one started in the margins of my PhD student's final thesis draft: his avionics models were trained once and evaluated once, but the data they describe spans decades. What catastrophic forgetting is and where the standard story comes from, which remedies I trust and why, how to read the field's numbers with care, a reading path through the literature, and a two-minute experiment you can run on a laptop.
